3. Problem statement and value proposition (rewritten)
================================================================================

Many Iranian macro sites sit on aged batteries and face 1.5–4 h scheduled cuts. Today a site runs everything until the battery dies, then goes dark. We propose a forecast-driven, safety-shielded controller that:
(a) trims energy at low traffic without hurting QoS, and
(b) during a cut, degrades gracefully — shedding capacity layers in a controlled order so the coverage layer (voice, SMS, emergency calls) stays alive longer — then recovers without a recharge shock.

Headline KPIs (in priority order):
1. Service-hours gained during cuts (both any-service and full-service reported).
2. QoS violations per day (sleep-induced congestion steps).
3. Rejected/Dropped Load during cuts (When traffic surges +15% and capacity is shed, the twin actively tracks dropped connections. We own the pitch: "Dropping non-critical data to guarantee emergency voice/SMS uptime").
4. Incremental energy saved vs a vendor-feature baseline (not vs zero saving).
5. Battery temperature-hours above 40 °C.
6. Hardware Fatigue / Toggle Cycles (Tracking OPEX: High-frequency cycling wears out RF relays and creates thermal stress. Energy saved must not cost more in hardware degradation).
7. Shield veto count and reason breakdown.

Honest baseline:
Vendors already ship energy-saving features. Ericsson's Micro Sleep Tx cut radio power ~15% on stc's LTE network [SRC], and engineers on a public forum describe the NR version as a basic feature enabled by default [SRC]. Therefore claim incremental savings (carrier-level, forecast-driven) on top of symbol/PA-level features, never "15% from nothing".

4.1 Site power model (replaces Section 29)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

EARTH / ETSI TR 36.887 macro reference:
- N_TRX = 6, P0 = 130 W, Delta_p = 4.7, P_max = 20 W, P_sleep = 75 W [SRC].
- Computed values:
  Full load (L=1.0): 1,344 W
  Idle load (L=0+):  780 W (58% of full)
  Sleep state:       450 W (58% of idle => 42% saving at zero load) [TEST]

Your 1.8 kW static / 3.0 kW full macro site matches this 58–60% static ratio. Keep the site totals, but replace the quadratic formula with the standard linear EARTH structure:

P_site = P_fixed + sum_c [ P0_c + Delta_c * L_c ]          (carrier c active, load L_c in [0,1])
P_site includes sum_c P_sleep_c = kappa * P0_c             (for sleeping carriers, kappa = 75/130 = 0.58 [SRC])

Calibrated explicit Power Tiers (GSM/3G voice layer + LTE capacity):
- P_fixed = 0.35 kW (BBU, transport, aux DC)
- P0 = 0.50 kW, Delta = 0.35 kW per LTE carrier
- GSM_FULL = 0.55 kW (6 TRX), GSM_MIN = 0.30 kW (3 TRX BCCH-only)
- 3GPP TR 38.864 sleep states are mapped via kappa factors. ES_SLEEP (kappa=0.58), OUTAGE_OFF (kappa=0).
Explicit Tiers:
- Tier 4 (FULL): GSM_FULL + 3 LTE carriers (3.450 kW at L=1)
- Tier 3 (ANCHOR+1): GSM_FULL + 2 LTE carriers (2.890 kW at L=1, 1 asleep)
- Tier 2 (BASIC): GSM_FULL + 1 LTE carrier (2.330 kW at L=1, 2 asleep)
- Tier 1 (SURVIVAL): GSM_MIN + 0 LTE carriers (0.650 kW, outage mode)
- Tier 0 (OFF): 0 kW (BLVD tripped)

Linear in load (no quadratic). A waking carrier draws >= P0 while serving nothing.
Wake-Up Signaling Tax: When a carrier wakes, hundreds of UEs perform simultaneous RRC connection handovers. This creates a transient baseband processing spike. The model adds a +10% max power penalty for the first 5 minutes after any wake action.
Deep carrier shutdown kappa below 0.58 is [UNVERIFIED] vendor-specific; sweep kappa in {0.30, 0.45, 0.58} and report sensitivity instead of fixing one number.
Hardware-to-Carrier Topology: Power savings assume independent radio modules. If a capacity carrier shares a wideband RRU (Remote Radio Unit) with the active anchor carrier, the RRU cannot power down. In `shared_rru=True` mode, sleeping capacity yields only ~15% savings (PA bias) instead of full 42% baseband savings.
AC cooling is mains-powered: 0 W on battery; optional DC fan draws 0.05–0.1 kW [ASSUMP].
5G n78 massive-MIMO radios are far hungrier; keep the PoC at 1 anchor + 2 capacity LTE layers and treat 5G as a documented future extension.

4.2 Battery model (replaces Section 26)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Chemistry is a parameter. Iranian legacy sites are mostly VRLA in practice [ASSUMP — confirm with the operator]; LFP is the modern replacement option. Support both.

Battery specifications:
- 15S LFP telecom packs: nominal 48 V, boost 53.5–54 V, float 52.5–54 V; 16S: 51.2 V nominal, float 56–57.6 V [SRC].
- Rectifier LLVD ≈ 43.2 V [SRC]. BLVD ≈ 42.0–43.0 V.
- Peukert equation:
  drain = I * (I / I_ref)^(n - 1)
  where I_ref = C / 10 (standard 10-hour discharge rating current).
  Lead-acid exponent n typically 1.1–1.3 [SRC]; LFP n ≈ 1.0–1.05 [ASSUMP].
  Halving load gives x2.06 runtime (LFP n=1.05) and x2.32 runtime (VRLA n=1.2) [TEST] — slightly better than linear due to reduced Peukert loss.

State of Health (SOH) & Sudden Death Simulation:
SOH is a first-class input, because worn batteries are the documented Iranian failure mode [SRC].
- Sweep SOH in {100%, 60%, 40%, 25%}.
- Raise Peukert exponent to n ≈ 1.15 for aged LFP and n ≈ 1.25 for aged VRLA [ASSUMP].
- Sudden Death: Aged VRLA batteries do not follow a perfect Peukert curve down to 15%. Sulfated cells collapse rapidly under load. The model injects stochastic rapid voltage drops for low-SOH batteries, ensuring the shield catches hardware failure and not just math timers.
- Internal resistance (15 mOhm bank).
- Thermal mass (corrected): A 200Ah 48V VRLA bank weighs ~300 kg (C ≈ 240 kJ/K). LFP weighs ~100 kg (C ≈ 100 kJ/K). Using 60 kJ/K artificially accelerates heating 4x.
- Grid-Recovery Brownout Protection: Upon grid return, deeply discharged batteries will pull max bulk charge (e.g., 0.2C = 40A = ~2.1kW), spiking total site load to 5+ kW. If an entire district recharges simultaneously, it trips local breakers. The simulator implements Rectifier Current Limiting (trickle charge at 0.05C for the first 30 minutes of grid return) to prevent secondary blackouts.


Aging & DoD Damage:
- VRLA cycle life plummets with Depth of Discharge (DoD). A discharge to 80% DoD yields ~300 cycles; to 30% DoD yields ~1270 cycles.
- VRLA float life halves every 8°C over 25°C. The twin implements both cycle damage (N(d) = N80*(0.8/d)^k) and Arrhenius calendar damage.

4.3 Thermal model (replaces Section 27 and Section 34.5)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What the real thermal risks are [SRC]:
- Lead-acid life halves for roughly every 7–10 °C above 25 °C (IEEE 484 cites 8 °C; literature ranges 7–10 °C). Cabinet-internal battery temperature is typically ~2 °C above shelter ambient.
- VRLA thermal runaway is a float/charge phenomenon (rising float current causes self-heating, which further increases current); exposure above ~40 °C is associated with electrolyte dry-out. It is NOT driven by discharge I^2*R.
- Therefore the dangerous moment is RECOVERY (grid returns, hot shelter, high recharge current) — not the outage itself.

Two-node thermal model:
1. Battery node: I^2*R heating (+ charge-phase exothermic heating for VRLA) exchanges heat with shelter air.
2. Shelter node: shelter air temperature is a state, heated by in-shelter equipment (BBU, transport, rectifier loss) and by the battery; passive heat loss only when AC cooling is off.

Crucial physical insight:
With Remote Radio Units (RRUs) mounted on the tower, their heat is rejected directly into outside air. Shedding RRU carriers barely changes in-shelter heat:
- Test run: 410 W in-shelter heat (all carriers active) drops to 380 W (anchor only).
- Peak shelter air temperature after 3 h at 40 °C ambient: 48.4 °C (full) vs 47.8 °C (shed) [TEST, assumptions UA = 40 W/K, C = 250 kJ/K].
- Conclusion: Shedding radio carriers changes shelter heat only slightly. The overwhelming value of load shedding is EXTENDING RUNTIME (AUTONOMY), not cabinet refrigeration.

Thermal rule (replaces naive "45 °C survival"):
- Derate at measured battery/shelter T >= 40 °C (VRLA) / 45 °C (LFP).
- Critical emergency shutdown at 50 °C.
- On recovery: cap initial recharge current and apply temperature-compensated float charging to prevent thermal runaway.

5.4 Forecaster feature governance and label policy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The forecaster must predict organic offered demand, not observed traffic after controller actions.

Allowed input features:
- Past offered traffic/load history.
- Lag features: 0, 1, 2, 3, 6, 12, 36, 144, 288 steps.
- Rolling mean/max over 1h, 6h, 24h windows.
- Same-time-last-day and same-time-last-week values.
- Time-of-day cyclical encodings.
- Day-of-week encodings.
- Iranian calendar flags:
    Friday weekend,
    Thursday half-day,
    national holidays,
    Ramadan regime flag.
- Ambient temperature (optional).
- Scheduled outage flag only if published in advance.

Forbidden input features:
- battery_soc_percent
- battery_temp_c
- battery_current_a
- battery_voltage_v
- site_power_dc_kw
- site_power_ac_kw
- active_carriers
- sleeping_carriers
- sleep_mode_level
- shield action history
- unscheduled grid_down_now if not known before prediction time
- future actual traffic
- future actual grid status

Rationale:
Battery, power, and carrier-state features are downstream consequences of traffic and controller actions. Feeding them into the forecaster creates target leakage, circular feedback, and label corruption.

Outage context policy:
- If a scheduled blackout is known in advance, the forecaster may use:
    scheduled_outage_flag
    expected_outage_start_in_minutes
    expected_outage_duration_minutes
- If the outage is unexpected, the forecaster must not receive future grid failure information.
- Unexpected outages are handled reactively by the Safety Shield's emergency wake and survival ladder.

Label policy:
traffic_state_label meanings:
    0 = organic traffic, normal or battery-backed operation with real users.
    1 = forced zero/reduced traffic due to shutdown, capacity shed, or forced outage.
    5 = demand surge caused by fixed-line/Wi-Fi outage.

Training rule:
- Label 0 is valid organic demand.
- Label 5 is also valid organic demand because the surge is real user demand migrating from fixed networks.
- Label 1 must not be treated as natural low demand.
- For offered-load training, target should represent:
    Organic Load + Rejected Load
  where rejected load is the demand that would have existed but could not be served due to shedding.

This prevents the Observer Effect:
    AI sleeps capacity -> observed traffic drops -> AI learns demand is low -> AI sleeps more.

6. Forecasting design (replaces Sections 32–33)
================================================================================

Target & Causal AI Confusion (The Observer Effect):
Next-60-minute peak offered load at the site level, expressed in carrier-capacity units (total offered volume / single-carrier capacity). PRB utilization of an individual carrier is derived, never the prediction target.
Crucially, the forecaster uses Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) or separates organic traffic drops from sleep-induced traffic drops. Training exclusively on post-deployment telemetry where the network slept creates an "Observer Effect" self-fulfilling prophecy (the AI sleeps capacity -> observes lower traffic -> thinks demand is low -> sleeps more). The model must predict what the traffic *would have been* (Offered Load) to prevent causal confusion.

Model ladder (build in this exact order, stop when requirements are met):
1. Profile-q95 per (weekday, hour-of-day): The fair baseline. Test: pinball loss 0.693, empirical coverage 0.840 (under-covers) [TEST].
2. Quantile Gradient Boosting (LightGBM): Trained with quantile loss (alpha=0.95), using lag features (lag0, 1, 2, 3, 6, 12, 36, 144, 288), rolling mean/max, and same-time-last-week peak. Test: pinball loss 0.489, empirical coverage 0.935 [TEST].
3. Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) or Quantile LSTM: Explored ONLY if LightGBM fails to meet safety coverage on a held-out blocked time split with a paired statistical test. Note: A naive "seasonal naive" baseline scored 1.152 pinball loss because it blindly copies last week's random spikes — do not use seasonal naive as a strawman.

Calibration (replaces static x1.25 multiplier):
- EnbPI / Block-Conformal: The target (next-60-min peak, evaluated every 5 mins) creates massively autocorrelated, overlapping rolling windows. Standard split-conformal assumes IID data and mathematically collapses here. We must use Ensemble Predictive Inference (EnbPI) or Block-Conformal methods designed for overlapping time-series to guarantee the 95% safety bound.
- Stacking static multipliers (q95 * 1.25 + conformal margin = 32.4% bound) is over-conservative and blocks valid energy-saving sleep decisions [TEST].
- Implement rolling/adaptive conformal calibration (recalibrate over sliding window of N days, widen margin on miss streaks).
- Implement drift alarm: an uncalibrated model's coverage drops from 95% to 72% under a +30% evening shift (e.g., Ramadan) [TEST].

Evaluation metrics:
- Pinball loss (quantile loss at alpha=0.95).
- Empirical coverage broken down by regime (normal, random spike, Ramadan, post-outage recovery).
- False Sleep Risk (FSR): rate at which sleep is authorized when actual peak exceeds capacity.
- Decision-focused frontier: kWh saved vs QoS violation-steps.
- Drop MAPE (near-zero night loads produce division-by-zero explosions).
- Validate strictly using blocked chronological time splits, never random train/test splits.

7. Control architecture and the O-RAN mapping (replaces Sections 11–12)
================================================================================

Verified O-RAN standard pattern [SRC]:
1. Non-RT RIC / SMO: The Energy Saving rApp monitors performance management counters over O1.
2. A1 Interface: The rApp sends an A1 Policy to a Traffic Steering xApp in the Near-RT RIC, instructing it to steer active UEs from capacity carriers to the anchor carrier.
3. O1 Interface: Once users are migrated, the rApp issues carrier sleep/deactivation via O1 Configuration Management (CM).
(Demonstrated by ONF SMaRT-5G, Rimedo Labs, and O-RAN SC end-to-end tests based on O-RAN WG1 NESUC specifications).

Carrier sleep/wake on a minutes timescale is fundamentally a Non-RT / O1 action, NOT an E2 sub-millisecond control action.

Corrections to v2.1:
- The previous E2 JSON message (`energySavingControl: sleep_capacity_carrier`) is not a standard E2SM message. Label it explicitly as a "PoC Message Abstraction". E2SM-RC support for carrier power states is vendor-dependent [UNVERIFIED].
- The A1 `energy_optimization` policy schema is a custom PoC structure. State: "Custom PoC policy type; production deployment would use standard A1 Traffic Steering / QoS targets or O1 CM".

Deployment path for the O-RAN Transition in Iran:
While current Iranian networks run legacy proprietary EMS, this solution is pitched as a bridge to the future: providing O-RAN-style Service Management and Orchestration (SMO) intelligence today. The Digital Twin acts as the foundational SMO layer for network modernization.
- Phase 0 (Advisory): The digital twin acts as a decision-support tool, outputting recommended sleep schedules and outage survival ladders for operator human approval.
- Phase 1 (Schedule Push): Pre-calculated time-window parameters are pushed via existing Northbound APIs as an interim step.
- Phase 2 (Closed Loop SMO): Real-time automated control leveraging native O1/A1 interfaces as Iranian operators transition to open, modernized O-RAN architectures.

Autonomy gating:
All phases pass through the identical deterministic safety shield. Shadow-mode logging is required before any command authorization.

8. Safety Arbiter: Priority Lattice (replaces Safety shield v2)
================================================================================


The arbiter has been redesigned as a mathematically formal Priority Lattice (UB/LB clamp).
It calculates Upper Bounds (UB) based on safety constraints and Lower Bounds (LB) based on service requirements.
final = clamp(proposal, LB, UB). If LB > UB, UB (Safety) wins.

Upper Bounds (Safety - local site agent):
- Backhaul down -> UB = 2 (BASIC — sheds capacity carriers without power-cycling anchor/voice layers off; see Section 8.10)
- Battery/Thermal Critical (effective SOC < 15%, V < 45.0V, T >= 50C) or Lease Expired on Battery -> UB = 1 (SURVIVAL)

Lower Bounds (Service - QoS):
- Grid Up -> LB = 2 (BASIC anchor floor), or LB = 4 (FULL) on Lease Expired (Dead-Man's Switch)
- Forecast / Emergency Wake -> LB = required capacity tier
- Hold gates (stale telemetry > 15m, dwell time < 15m, fatigue budget exhausted) -> LB = current state
- Grid Down -> LB = 1 (SURVIVAL floor)

Invariant Violation Tests [TEST]:
Randomized Arbiter states (trials=10000): conflict:UB-wins in 1486 cases. 0 UB or LB violations.

- Shield v2 (refined): 0 anchor-sleep violations, 0 capacity violations in 200,000 trials [TEST].

Iranian anchor carrier definition:
The lowest-frequency, widest-coverage carrier physically deployed on the tower (B8 900 MHz where present, otherwise B3 1800 MHz) plus the 2G/3G legacy voice layer; never a hypothetical 700/800 MHz LTE layer.

8.10 Shield v2.1 operational extensions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Effective SOC handling:
Because SOC estimation is unreliable on aged VRLA batteries under load, the shield uses a conservative effective SOC:

    effective_soc = measured_soc - soc_uncertainty_margin

Recommended uncertainty margins:
    VRLA: 10 percentage points [ASSUMP]
    LFP with BMS: 5 percentage points [ASSUMP]

Voltage backstop:
If battery voltage < 45.0V, the shield forces anchor-only mode immediately, regardless of SOC estimate.

LLVD/BLVD protection:
- LLVD warning/cutoff: approximately 43.2V.
- BLVD range: approximately 42.0V to 43.0V.
- Controlled shutdown must occur before permanent battery damage.

Hardware fatigue cap:
Each carrier has a hard cap of 4 sleep/wake cycles per 24-hour period.
If the cap is reached, the carrier remains frozen in its current safe state until the next operational day unless an emergency override is triggered.

Backhaul fate-sharing:
If backhaul state = DOWN, capacity carriers must not remain active.
The site must not waste battery power broadcasting an isolated LTE island.

Fail-static behavior:
If telemetry age > 15 minutes, the controller freezes the last verified safe state.
No sleep/wake action may be issued on stale data.

Emergency wake override:
If active PRB/load exceeds 85%, the minimum dwell time is bypassed and capacity carriers are awakened immediately, subject to physical wake-up latency.

Correlated-outage veto:
If neighboring sites share the same feeder and are offline, low battery, or degraded, the shield must not assume offload capacity exists.
Feeder overlap probability is configurable.
Default:
    feeder_overlap_probability = 0.5
Sensitivity sweep:
    0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00

9. Outage Planner: Receding-Horizon Reserve (replaces Outage Survival Protocol & RL)
================================================================================


Because Iranian blackouts are published in advance via the 'Bargh-e Man' app, we replace the speculative RL approach with a deterministic Receding-Horizon Reserve Policy.

1) Reserve enough HARD-floor energy to keep SURVIVAL (Tier 1) alive until the q90 (90th percentile) expected grid-return time.
2) Capacity tiers may only spend surplus energy above the SOFT floor (battery-life protection), and only what remains after the reserve.
3) Choose the highest tier affordable for the expected (q50) remaining outage.
Re-evaluated every step => tiers drop/rise as grid-return estimates change.

Example Planner decisions (T_q50=2.0h, T_q90=3.0h, margin=10%) [TEST]:
  E_soft=1.5kWh, E_hard=2.0kWh -> chooses Tier 1 (SURVIVAL)
  E_soft=3.0kWh, E_hard=8.0kWh -> chooses Tier 2 (BASIC)
  E_soft=6.0kWh, E_hard=8.0kWh -> chooses Tier 4 (FULL)

13. Judge Q&A (honest, defensible answers)
================================================================================

Q1: Why not just use a simple time-of-day clock?
A: In our multi-seed out-of-sample benchmark (`src/benchmark.py`, 9 unseen site-runs × 21 days), a static 00:00–06:00 timer caused 145–151 severe QoS violation steps while saving only 2.13–2.15% AC kWh because it cannot anticipate holiday variations, demand surges, or irregular traffic spikes. Meanwhile, a reactive threshold caused 694 violation steps at 15-min wake latency. Our Forecast + Conformal + Shield architecture saved 3.23–3.39% AC kWh with 0 controller-induced violations at 0-min wake latency (5 total = identical to Always-FULL physical spikes) and 15.4x fewer violations than reactive at 15-min wake latency, while actively managing blackout survival.

Q2: Modern base station vendors already include energy-saving features. What is novel here?
A: Vendors implement fast symbol-level and slot-level micro-sleep (e.g., Ericsson Micro Sleep Tx, saving ~15% radio power). Our solution operates at a higher architectural tier: it is carrier-level, forecast-driven, outage-aware, and O-RAN-aligned. It does not replace vendor micro-sleep; it stacks on top of it to manage macro carrier states and battery preservation.

Q3: All your simulation results are based on synthetic data. How can we trust them?
A: We are completely transparent about this: our results test physical mechanisms and internal consistency, not field telemetry. However, every equation is grounded in peer-reviewed literature: the power model is calibrated to ETSI TR 36.887, battery dynamics follow Peukert's law, and thermal limits conform to IEEE 484. All parameters are configurable and accompanied by sensitivity sweeps.

Q4: Is the battery thermal runaway story realistic during an outage?
A: We corrected this misconception from our early research: battery I^2*R discharge heating is only ~1–2% of site power, and Remote Radio Units reject heat outdoors on the tower. The true thermal risk for VRLA batteries is during grid RECOVERY (float current runaway in a hot shelter). The primary value of load shedding during an outage is not cooling — it is AUTONOMY (+40% uptime).

Q5: Legacy Iranian telecom networks use closed vendor EMS. How could this ever be deployed?
A: We define a realistic 3-phase deployment roadmap: Phase 0 is an offline Advisory Tool where operators review AI recommendations. Phase 1 pushes daily schedule windows through vendor EMS Northbound APIs. Phase 2 enables closed-loop control only when open O-RAN interfaces become available.

Q6: What happens if the AI model makes a disastrously incorrect forecast?
A: The AI never controls hardware directly. A hard-coded, deterministic safety shield inspects every proposal. The shield enforces 7 absolute invariants: it never sleeps the anchor carrier, evaluates target capacity directly, enforces 15-minute hysteresis, and fails static if telemetry drops.

Q7: What if neighboring towers are also overloaded?
A: In Iran, rolling blackouts are district-wide and feeder-level. Our safety shield includes a Correlated-Outage Veto: if neighboring towers share the same feeder and are offline, the system never assumes neighbors can absorb offloaded traffic.

Q8: Does shedding capacity carriers degrade user experience during a blackout?
A: Yes, and we state this openly. When the grid fails and traffic surges by 15%, shedding capacity layers reduces data throughput. However, the Graceful Degradation Ladder trades full-service hours (-58%) to gain +16% to +19% additional any-service hours. In an emergency, maintaining voice calls and SMS is far more vital than streaming high-definition video.

Q9: Why not simply replace the worn-out batteries with new ones?
A: Telecom executives in Iran have publicly stated that capital constraints and currency devaluation prevent nationwide battery replacement across 110,000+ towers. Software-driven graceful degradation extends the operational life of existing, degraded batteries today without requiring immediate capital expenditure.

Q10: What is the financial savings in Iranian Rials?
A: Electricity in Iran is among the cheapest in the world due to state subsidies, making pure kWh savings economically negligible. The true business value lies in infrastructure preservation: preventing permanent battery destruction, avoiding diesel refueling logistics, minimizing SLA regulatory fines, and maintaining emergency connectivity.
