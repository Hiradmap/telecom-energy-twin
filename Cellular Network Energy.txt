## 1. Executive Summary

This project builds an AI-driven energy efficiency system for cellular base stations.

The system predicts future cellular traffic load and safely transitions base station hardware into low-power states during low-demand periods. Its key differentiator is the **Thermal Survival Protocol**, which protects UPS batteries during grid blackouts by shedding hardware load before the battery overheats or reaches dangerous discharge levels.

Because legacy telecom equipment uses closed Element Management Systems, direct live deployment is not realistic today. Therefore, the project is built as a **Digital Twin Proof of Concept** emulating an O-RAN architecture.

The core system is:

```text
Historical telemetry
    ↓
Feature engineering
    ↓
Traffic peak forecasting model
    ↓
Heuristic safety shield
    ↓
Optional shielded reinforcement learning policy
    ↓
Digital twin simulator
    ↓
Energy savings, KPI impact, battery thermal impact
```

The final PoC demonstrates:

1. Traffic forecasting using quantile or peak-based prediction.
2. Safe sleep/wake decisions for capacity carriers.
3. Preservation of the anchor coverage carrier.
4. Battery thermal protection during grid failure.
5. Energy savings estimation.
6. KPI penalty simulation.
7. O-RAN-style policy and control emulation.
8. Operator dashboard for visualization and pitch.

The strongest pitch statement is:

> The AI does not shut down the network. It forecasts risk. A deterministic safety shield authorizes only hardware states that satisfy coverage, capacity, neighbor load, and battery thermal constraints.

## 2. Core Value Proposition

The project creates value in three layers.

### 2.1 Operational Expenditure Reduction

Base stations consume significant power even at low traffic. By sleeping capacity carriers during low-load periods, the system reduces:

- Electricity cost.
- Cooling load.
- Hardware wear.
- Carbon footprint.

### 2.2 Infrastructure Protection

During grid failure, the site runs on battery. High load causes high discharge current, which causes internal battery heating:

```text
P_heat = I² * R_internal
```

By reducing load, the system reduces current and heat generation.

If load is halved, current is halved, and heat generation is reduced by approximately four times.

This is the core of the Thermal Survival Protocol.

### 2.3 O-RAN Future Readiness

The system is designed as an O-RAN rApp. It cannot directly control legacy closed EMS hardware, but it is ready for future open RAN deployments.

The pitch positioning is:

> Legacy networks cannot accept this today because they are closed. The system is built as an O-RAN-ready Digital Twin, waiting for the inevitable hardware and interface upgrade.

## 3. The Five Original Engineering Constraints

These constraints define the difficulty of the project.

### 3.1 The API Wall

Legacy telecom equipment often uses closed EMS systems.

They may not expose:

- Open telemetry.
- Open control APIs.
- Real-time power state commands.
- Battery telemetry.
- Carrier sleep commands.

Therefore, live deployment is not feasible without vendor cooperation.

**Mitigation:** Build a Digital Twin PoC using O-RAN principles.

---

### 3.2 Reinforcement Learning Loopholes

A live RL agent is dangerous because RL explores actions.

If the reward only optimizes energy savings, the agent may learn:

> “The best way to save energy is to shut down the entire tower.”

**Mitigation:** Use supervised forecasting first, deterministic safety shield second, and simulator-only RL third.

---

### 3.3 Data Poisoning

Scheduled blackouts or forced shutdowns can poison the training data.

If traffic drops to zero every day at 16:00 due to grid failure, the AI may incorrectly learn:

> “16:00 is naturally low traffic.”

Then it may recommend sleep when the grid is stable.

**Mitigation:** Label outage periods, mask forced-zero rows, and include grid/outage flags.

---

### 3.4 Cell Breathing

Reducing transmit power shrinks coverage.

If the anchor carrier power is reduced, users at the cell edge lose service.

**Mitigation:** Do not reduce anchor transmit power. Sleep capacity carriers while keeping the anchor carrier active.

---

### 3.5 Wake-up Latency

Hardware does not wake instantly.

If a traffic spike occurs while hardware is sleeping, users may experience congestion or drops.

**Mitigation:** Predict peak load, use predictive wake-up, hysteresis timers, and conservative thresholds.

## 6. Physical Telecom Site Model

A macro cell site consists of:

```text
Grid AC
Diesel generator
Automatic transfer switch
Rectifier system
-48V DC busbar
Battery bank
Baseband unit / O-DU / O-CU
Remote Radio Head / O-RU
Antennas
Backhaul
Cooling system
```

### 6.1 Power Flow

Normal grid mode:

```text
Grid AC
    ↓
Rectifier
    ↓
-48V DC bus
    ↓
Telecom load + battery float charge
```

Grid failure mode:

```text
Battery bank
    ↓
-48V DC bus
    ↓
Telecom load
```

Generator mode:

```text
Generator AC
    ↓
Rectifier
    ↓
-48V DC bus
    ↓
Telecom load + battery recharge
```

### 6.2 Important Voltages

For a nominal 48V telecom plant:

```text
Float voltage: approximately 53.5V to 54.4V
LLVD threshold: approximately 46.8V
BLVD threshold: approximately 43V to 44V
```

BLVD means Battery Low Voltage Disconnect.

When BLVD triggers, the site shuts down to protect the battery.

## 7. Site Operational States

The Digital Twin should model these states.

### 7.1 NORMAL_OPS

Grid is stable. Traffic is normal. All required carriers are active.

### 7.2 ENERGY_SAVING

Traffic is low. Secondary capacity carriers may sleep.

### 7.3 PREDICTIVE_WAKE

Forecast predicts an upcoming traffic spike. Hardware is awakened early.

### 7.4 GRID_OUTAGE_BATTERY

Grid is lost. Site runs on battery. Energy saving becomes survival-oriented.

### 7.5 THERMAL_SURVIVAL_ANCHOR_ONLY

Battery temperature or SOC crosses danger threshold. System sheds load and keeps only the anchor carrier.

### 7.6 BLVD_SHUTDOWN

Battery voltage is critically low. Controlled shutdown occurs.

### 7.7 RECOVERY

Grid returns. Battery recharges. System gradually restores capacity carriers.

## 8. Sleep Mode Strategy

The safest strategy is:

```text
Keep the anchor coverage carrier active.
Sleep secondary capacity carriers.
Do not reduce anchor transmit power.
```

### 8.1 Anchor Carrier

The anchor carrier should usually be a low-frequency coverage layer.

Examples:

```text
700 MHz
800 MHz
900 MHz
```

Low frequencies provide:

- Larger coverage radius.
- Better building penetration.
- Better cell-edge survival.
- Emergency call continuity.

### 8.2 Capacity Carriers

Capacity carriers may be higher frequency layers.

Examples:

```text
1800 MHz
2100 MHz
2600 MHz
3500 MHz
```

These provide high throughput but smaller coverage.

They are the primary candidates for sleep.

## 9. Sleep Modes and Latencies

Accepted high-level sleep modes:

| Sleep Mode | What Is Disabled | Power Saving | Wake Latency | Risk |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Symbol-level micro sleep | PA during empty symbols | 5%–15% | < 1 ms | Very low |
| Cell DTX | PA between broadcast signals | 15%–30% | 1–10 ms | Low |
| MIMO muting | Secondary RF chains | 15%–25% | 10–50 ms | Medium |
| SCell dormancy | Capacity carrier scheduling | 30%–40% | 50–200 ms | Low |
| SSB-less dormancy | Capacity carrier broadcast and scheduling | 35%–50% | 100–200 ms | Low |
| Deep carrier shutdown | Carrier hardware state | 40%–60% | 5–15 seconds conservative | Medium |
| Full cell shutdown | Entire site | ~100% | minutes | Extreme |

### Important Correction

The control message may arrive quickly, but the physical hardware may take longer to become fully active.

Therefore the simulator should separate:

```text
message_latency
hardware_action_latency
service_ready_latency
```

Recommended simulation classes:

```text
FAST_CARRIER_DORMANCY:
    saving = 30%
    wake_latency = 0.5 seconds

DEEP_CARRIER_SHUTDOWN:
    saving = 45%
    wake_latency = 10 seconds
```

## 10. Cell Breathing and Coverage Safety

Reducing transmit power shrinks the coverage area.

Approximate coverage shrinkage using urban macro path-loss assumptions:

| Power Reduction | Approximate Radius Shrinkage |
|---:|---:|
| 3 dB | ~17% |
| 6 dB | ~30% |
| 10 dB | ~44% |

Therefore, the project avoids transmit power reduction as the main sleep mechanism.

Instead:

```text
Sleep capacity carriers.
Keep anchor carrier at full power.
Maintain geographic coverage perimeter.
```

This preserves emergency service availability.

## 17. Traffic State Labels

| Value | Meaning |
|---:|---|

| `2` | Forced zero/reduced traffic due to manual shutdown |

## 26. Synthetic Battery Simulation

Use a LiFePO4 model by default.

### 26.1 Battery Parameters

```text
battery_capacity_ah = 200
battery_nominal_voltage = 48
battery_full_voltage = 54
battery_cutoff_voltage = 44
internal_resistance_ohms = 0.015
peukert_exponent = 1.05
```

### 26.2 Voltage Approximation

Simple linear model for MVP:

```text
battery_voltage_v = 44 + 10 * battery_soc_percent / 100
```

Examples:

```text
SOC 100% -> 54V
SOC 50%  -> 49V
SOC 0%   -> 44V
```

### 26.2.1 Improved Piecewise Voltage Model

LiFePO4 cells have a characteristic flat voltage plateau between 20% and 80% SOC. The simple linear model overestimates current draw at mid-SOC and underestimates it at low SOC, which distorts thermal calculations.

For best version, use a 3-segment piecewise linear approximation:

```text
SOC = battery_soc_percent / 100

If SOC > 0.80:
    battery_voltage_v = 51.5 + (SOC - 0.80) * 12.5

If 0.20 <= SOC <= 0.80:
    battery_voltage_v = 49.5 + (SOC - 0.20) * 3.33

If SOC < 0.20:
    battery_voltage_v = 44.0 + (SOC / 0.20) * 5.5
```

Examples:

```text
SOC 100% -> 54.0V
SOC 80%  -> 51.5V
SOC 50%  -> 50.5V
SOC 20%  -> 49.5V
SOC 10%  -> 46.75V
SOC 0%   -> 44.0V
```

This produces more realistic current and heat estimates, especially during the critical low-SOC region where thermal survival decisions matter most.

### 26.3 Current Calculation

```text
battery_current_a = site_power_dc_watts / battery_voltage_v
```

### 26.4 SOC Discharge Approximation

```text
soc_drop_per_5min =
    (battery_current_a / battery_capacity_ah)
    * (5 / 60)
    * 100
```

Then:

```text
battery_soc_percent = battery_soc_percent - soc_drop_per_5min
```

Apply Peukert correction for higher accuracy.

## 27. Battery Thermal Model

Use lumped thermal model:

```text
T_next = T_current + Δt * (P_heat - P_cooling) / (m * Cp)
```

Where:

```text
P_heat = I² * R_internal
P_cooling = hA * (T_current - T_ambient)
```

Recommended defaults:

```text
battery_mass_kg = 60
specific_heat_capacity_J_per_kgC = 1000
hA_cooling_on_W_per_K = 10
hA_cooling_off_W_per_K = 2
ambient_temp_c = 30
delta_t_seconds = 300
```

Example:

```text
Site power = 3 kW
Battery voltage = 48V
Current = 62.5A
R_internal = 0.015Ω

P_heat = 62.5² * 0.015
       ≈ 58.6W
```

If load is reduced to 1.5 kW:

```text
Current = 31.25A
P_heat = 31.25² * 0.015
       ≈ 14.6W
```

Heat generation drops by about 75%.

## 29. Base Station Power Model

Use ETSI-inspired quadratic model:

```text
P_dc = P_static + P_dynamic_max * (a * L + b * L²)
```

Where:

```text
L = prb_dl_percent / 100
a = 0.8
b = 0.2
```

Recommended frozen values for medium macro site:

```text
P_static_dc_kw = 1.8
P_dynamic_max_dc_kw = 1.2
```

Therefore:

```text
P_dc = 1.8 + 1.2 * (0.8 * L + 0.2 * L²)
```

Rectifier efficiency:

```text
rectifier_efficiency = 0.96
P_ac = P_dc / rectifier_efficiency
```

Example:

```text
PRB = 50%
L = 0.5

dynamic_factor = 0.8 * 0.5 + 0.2 * 0.25
               = 0.45

P_dc = 1.8 + 1.2 * 0.45
     = 2.34 kW

P_ac = 2.34 / 0.96
     = 2.44 kW
```

### 29.1 Carrier Sleep Power Reduction

When capacity carriers are put to sleep, the site power consumption is reduced.

The power model during carrier sleep is:

```text
P_dc_sleeping = P_static * (1 - N_sleeping * carrier_sleep_fraction)
              + P_dynamic_max * (1 - N_sleeping * carrier_dynamic_fraction)
              * (a * L_anchor + b * L_anchor²)
```

For simplified implementation, use:

```text
P_saved_per_carrier_fast_dormancy = 0.30 * P_dynamic_max
P_saved_per_carrier_deep_shutdown = 0.45 * P_dynamic_max
```

Example for one capacity carrier in fast dormancy:

```text
P_saved = 0.30 * 1.2 = 0.36 kW
P_dc_reduced = P_dc_normal - 0.36
```

Example for one capacity carrier in deep shutdown:

```text
P_saved = 0.45 * 1.2 = 0.54 kW
P_dc_reduced = P_dc_normal - 0.54
```

For a site with 2 capacity carriers both in deep shutdown:

```text
P_saved = 2 * 0.54 = 1.08 kW
P_dc_reduced = P_dc_normal - 1.08
```

The minimum site power when only the anchor carrier is active:

```text
P_dc_anchor_only = P_static * anchor_static_fraction + P_dynamic_anchor

P_static * anchor_static_fraction = 1.8 * 0.50 = 0.90 kW
P_dynamic_anchor = P_dynamic_max * 0.33 * (a * L + b * L²)
```

Recommended frozen values:

```text
anchor_static_fraction = 0.50
anchor_dynamic_fraction = 0.33
total_capacity_carriers = 2
```

This means the anchor carrier alone consumes roughly 0.9 to 1.3 kW depending on load, compared to the full-site range of 1.8 to 3.0 kW.

### 29.2 Cooling System Power Model

The cooling system is a significant and often overlooked load.

```text
P_cooling_active_kw = 0.4
P_cooling_fan_only_kw = 0.1
P_cooling_off_kw = 0.0
```

Cooling state depends on operational mode:

```text
NORMAL_OPS:                  cooling = active
ENERGY_SAVING:               cooling = active or fan-only
GRID_OUTAGE_BATTERY:         cooling = fan-only (to conserve battery)
THERMAL_SURVIVAL:            cooling = fan-only (battery conservation)
BLVD_SHUTDOWN:               cooling = off
```

The cooling power should be added to the total site power:

```text
P_total_dc = P_dc_telecom + P_cooling
```

During grid outage with fan-only cooling, the reduced cooling capacity is already captured by the lower cooling coefficient:

```text
hA_cooling_on = 10 W/K    (active cooling)
hA_cooling_off = 2 W/K    (fan-only or natural convection)
```

### 29.3 Generator Model

When the grid fails, a diesel generator may or may not start.

```text
generator_start_delay_minutes = 10
generator_start_failure_probability = 0.15
generator_fuel_autonomy_hours = 8
```

Generator behavior in simulation:

```text
1. Grid fails.
2. Site switches to battery immediately.
3. Generator start attempt begins after generator_start_delay_minutes.
4. With probability (1 - generator_start_failure_probability),
   generator starts and site transitions to generator mode.
5. With probability generator_start_failure_probability,
   generator fails and site remains on battery.
6. If generator starts, battery recharges and thermal pressure decreases.
7. If generator runs out of fuel, site falls back to battery.
```

Generator power capacity:

```text
generator_capacity_kw = 5.0
generator_efficiency = 0.85
```

This is enough to power the full site and recharge the battery simultaneously.

For the PoC, generator events should be configurable per simulation scenario to test both generator-success and generator-failure paths.

## 30. Energy Savings Calculation

Energy saved over time:

```text
E_saved = Σ (P_baseline - P_actual) * Δt
```

Where:

```text
Δt is in hours.
Power is in kW.
Energy is in kWh.
```

For 5-minute steps:

```text
Δt = 5 / 60 hours
```

## 31. Neighbor Cell Offload Model

Before sleeping a carrier, check neighbor capacity.

### 31.1 Formula

```text
displaced_prb =
    current_prb_of_cell_being_slept * offload_fraction

projected_neighbor_prb =
    neighbor_current_prb
    + displaced_prb * rf_penalty
```

Recommended:

```text
offload_fraction = 0.3
rf_penalty = 1.75
projected_neighbor_prb_limit = 70%
```

The offload_fraction of 0.3 means approximately 30% of the sleeping cell's traffic will spill to neighbors. This is a standard assumption for co-sited macro cells where most users are closer to the cell center and can be served by the anchor carrier without handover.

If:

```text
projected_neighbor_prb > 70%
```

then veto offload/sleep.

---

### 31.2 Handover Modeling

Do not assume instant offload.

Recommended simulation values:

```text
handover_preparation_time_seconds = 1 to 5
handover_execution_time_seconds = 0.1 to 1
handover_failure_probability = 0.01
offload_ramp_minutes = 3
```

## 32. AI Forecasting Design

The best version uses probabilistic peak forecasting.

### 32.1 Prediction Goal

Predict:

```text
next_60m_q95_prb_dl_percent
```

Then compute:

```text
next_60m_safe_peak_prb_dl_percent =
    next_60m_q95_prb_dl_percent * safety_margin
```

Recommended:

```text
safety_margin = 1.25
```

---

### 32.2 Model Architecture Options

Best option:

```text
Temporal Fusion Transformer
```

Fallback option:

```text
LSTM with quantile regression output
```

Reason:

- TFT handles multi-horizon forecasting well.
- TFT handles static covariates and time features well.
- LSTM is simpler and more stable for small teams.

For the best version:

```text
Use TFT if data and compute allow.
Use LSTM quantile as fallback.
```

---

### 32.5 Output

Recommended output:

```text

```

Primary safety output:

```text
q95
```

Optional direct output:

```text
next_60m_peak_prb_dl_percent
```

But quantile output is better.

---

### 32.6 Loss Function

Use quantile loss.

For quantile q:

```text
error = y_true - y_pred

if error >= 0:
    loss = q * error
else:
    loss = (1 - q) * (-error)
```

For q95:

```text
q = 0.95
```

This penalizes under-prediction more heavily than over-prediction.

Optional asymmetric spike penalty:

```text
If actual peak > predicted safe peak:
    add extra penalty
```

---

### 32.7 Conformal Prediction

Use a calibration set.

Workflow:

```text
1. Train model on training set.
2. Predict q95 on calibration set.
3. Compute residual:
   residual = actual_peak - predicted_q95
4. Take high percentile of residuals, e.g. 95%.
5. Use that as conformal_margin.
6. At inference:
   safe_bound = predicted_q95 + conformal_margin
```

This gives a stronger safety claim.

## 34. Heuristic Safety Engine

The safety engine is deterministic and hard-coded.

It must override AI and RL.

---

### 34.1 Normal Sleep Rule

Allow sleep only if:

```text
grid_status == 1
AND predicted_safe_peak < 25%
AND current_prb < 30%
AND neighbor_projected_prb < 70%
AND active_ue < safe_user_threshold
AND no hardware fault
AND no thermal emergency
AND hysteresis constraints satisfied
```

Recommended frozen value:

```text
safe_user_threshold = 50
```

This represents a low-traffic state for a medium urban macro cell. Below 50 active UEs, the anchor carrier alone can typically serve remaining users without congestion.

---

### 34.2 Wake Rule

Force wake if:

```text
predicted_safe_peak > 60%
OR current_prb > 75%
OR drop_call_rate > 1.5%
OR handover_success_rate < 95%
OR traffic spike detected
```

---

### 34.3 Predictive Wake Rule

If:

```text
predicted_safe_peak > 60% within next 10 minutes
```

then:

```text
wake capacity carriers now
```

Recommended:

```text
predictive_wake_lead_minutes = 10
```

---

### 34.4 Grid Failure Rule

If:

```text
grid_status == 0
```

then:

```text
Disable normal energy saving.
Enable survival mode.
Sleep non-essential capacity layers.
Keep anchor carrier active.
Monitor battery SOC and temperature.
```

---

### 34.5 Thermal Survival Rule

If:

```text
grid_status == 0
AND battery_temp_c > 45
```

or:

```text
battery_soc_percent < 15
```

then:

```text
Force anchor-only mode.
Shed capacity carriers.
Offload users only if neighbor capacity exists.
Reduce non-essential load.
Prepare controlled shutdown if BLVD is near.
```

---

### 34.6 Hysteresis Rules

```text
minimum_sleep_duration_minutes = 15
minimum_active_duration_minutes = 15
```

Prevent rapid state toggling.

---

### 34.7 Hard Constraints

The system must never:

```text
Sleep the anchor carrier during normal operation.
Offload users to an overloaded neighbor.
Ignore battery critical temperature.
Allow predicted load to exceed active capacity.
Use live RL actions without shield.
```

## 38. Known Holes and Issues

These are the current known weaknesses, risks, and unresolved items.

---

### 38.1 Exact O-RAN Message Support

**Issue:** Exact support for energy-saving control through E2 service models may be vendor-dependent.

**Risk:** Judges may ask for exact standardized fields.

**Mitigation:** Emulate O-RAN principles. State clearly that exact service model support is deployment-dependent.

**Research needed:**

- O-RAN WG2 A1 policy specifications.
- O-RAN WG3 E2SM-RC specifications.
- E2SM-KPM field definitions.
- Vendor support for energy-saving actions.

---

### 38.2 Wake-up Latency Realism

**Issue:** Some research values suggest 50–200 ms for carrier dormancy, but deeper hardware sleep may take seconds.

**Risk:** Overclaiming fast wake-up may weaken the defense.

**Mitigation:** Use two sleep classes:

```text
Fast dormancy
Deep carrier shutdown
```

Separate:

```text
message latency
hardware latency
service readiness latency
```

---

### 38.3 Sub-5-Minute Traffic Spikes

**Issue:** 5-minute counters may hide short spikes.

**Risk:** The AI may falsely recommend sleep before a spike.

**Mitigation:**

- Use q95 prediction.
- Add safety margin.
- Use conformal prediction.
- In synthetic twin, generate hidden 1-minute or 1-second traffic.
- Evaluate against hidden true peak.

---

### 38.4 Battery Thermal Coefficients

**Issue:** The thermal equation is correct, but exact coefficients are not known.

**Risk:** Thermal curve may look unrealistic.

**Mitigation:** Use configurable lumped parameters:

```text
battery_mass_kg = 60
specific_heat_capacity = 1000
hA_cooling_on = 10
hA_cooling_off = 2
ambient_temp_c = 30
```

State that these are simulation assumptions.

---

### 38.5 Battery Voltage Sag

**Issue:** Simple models assume constant 48V.

**Risk:** Current and heat estimates become inaccurate.

**Mitigation:** Use:

```text
V_bus = 44 + 10 * SOC
I = P / V
```

---

### 38.6 Legacy Site Telemetry

**Issue:** Legacy sites may not expose battery temperature or SOC.

**Risk:** Judges may ask how telemetry is obtained.

**Mitigation:** Say the PoC assumes a modernized O-RAN site or external battery telemetry gateway.

---

### 38.7 Real Blackout Behavior

**Issue:** Real regional blackout schedules and site survival behavior are unknown.

**Risk:** Simulation may not match real operator experience.

**Mitigation:** Make blackout profiles configurable. Show multiple scenarios.

---

### 38.8 Dataset Availability

**Issue:** Public datasets may lack PRB + battery + grid status.

**Risk:** No single real dataset covers the full problem.

**Mitigation:** Use standard-calibrated synthetic data. Document calibration sources.

---

### 38.9 Neighbor Overload Accuracy

**Issue:** Offloaded cell-edge users may consume more PRBs.

**Risk:** Sleep decision may overload neighbor cells.

**Mitigation:** Use RF penalty and projected neighbor PRB limit.

Recommended:

```text
rf_penalty = 1.75
max_projected_neighbor_prb = 70%
```

---

### 38.10 Handover Delay

**Issue:** Offload is not instantaneous.

**Risk:** Thermal Survival Mode may appear magically seamless.

**Mitigation:** Model bounded handover delay and small failure probability.

---

### 38.11 RL Value

**Issue:** RL may not outperform the heuristic engine.

**Risk:** Judges may ask why RL is needed.

**Mitigation:** Present RL as an optimizer trained inside the twin, not as a required controller.

The strongest answer:

> The heuristic engine is the safe baseline. RL is explored only inside the Digital Twin and only if it improves energy savings without violating safety constraints.

---

## 44. Judge Q&A

### Question 1: Why not use a simple timer?

**Answer:** Timers fail during holidays, emergencies, traffic spikes, and blackouts. Our system uses dynamic traffic forecasting and power-state awareness.

---

### Question 2: Why not deploy directly on live towers?

**Answer:** Legacy EMS systems are closed. We built the system as an O-RAN Digital Twin to prove the logic and prepare for open RAN deployment.

---

### Question 3: What prevents the AI from shutting down the network?

**Answer:** The AI does not directly control hardware. A deterministic safety shield enforces hard constraints. RL is simulator-only and shielded.

---

### Question 4: How do you handle sudden traffic spikes?

**Answer:** We predict upper-bound peak load, use safety margins, conformal bounds, predictive wake-up, and hysteresis.

---

### Question 5: How does load shedding help batteries?

**Answer:** Battery heat scales with current squared. Halving load approximately halves current and reduces heat generation by about four times.

---

### Question 6: What if neighbor cells overload?

**Answer:** The safety engine projects neighbor load using an RF penalty and vetoes offload if projected neighbor PRB exceeds 70%.

---

### Question 7: What if your synthetic data is unrealistic?

**Answer:** The synthetic data is calibrated from open traffic traces, ETSI-style power modeling, 3GPP-inspired KPI definitions, and battery datasheet parameters. All assumptions are configurable and disclosed.

---

### Question 8: Why not reduce transmit power?

**Answer:** Reducing transmit power causes cell breathing and drops cell-edge users. We sleep capacity carriers while keeping the anchor carrier active.

---

### Question 9: Is RL necessary?

**Answer:** No. The heuristic safety engine is the primary controller. RL is an optional optimization layer trained only inside the simulator.

---

### Question 10: What is the strongest technical contribution?

**Answer:** The Thermal Survival Protocol: using load shedding not only for energy savings, but for physical battery preservation during grid instability.
